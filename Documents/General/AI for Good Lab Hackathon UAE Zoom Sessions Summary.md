## 🎯 Objective & Core Requirements

The goal is to create crowdsourced, open-knowledge-based AI solutions and agents mapped to real-world country policies and strategies. Solutions must comprise two main components:

1. **Part 1: Technical Solution & Use Case:** Implement an agentic AI solution/model within specific domains (e.g., healthcare, education, agriculture, 5G/6G, public services).
    
2. **Part 2: Policy & Standards Mapping:** Map your solution to open, publicly available documents (regulations, AI strategies, cybersecurity standards, data protection guidelines). _Note: Do not use confidential materials; only use openly published documents._

## 💻 Submission Deliverables

Teams must submit the following package by November 10:

- **Technical Report:** Maximum 5 pages using the provided template (must include team member contact details and description of use cases).
    
- **GitHub Repository:** Source code for your agents and vectorized knowledge bases (Markdown, JSON, etc.).
    
- **Knowledge Base:** Mapped policies, regulations, and strategy documents.
    
- **Demo Video:** Maximum 7 minutes showing interactive scenario flows and agent auditing/reasoning.

Here is a summary of the additional details and Q&A from this `second` mentoring session of the **AI for Good Lab Hackathon (UAE Edition)**:

## 🎯 Key Clarifications & Guidelines

- **Focus Areas:** Teams are encouraged to develop Agentic AI solutions in four key domains: **Education, Healthcare, Government Services, and Telecom**.
    
- **Demo Video Requirements:** While demo videos were initially requested for the first round, submission for the demo video has been adjusted/relaxed for the first-round evaluation due to time constraints, but it will be required for shortlisted teams entering the second round.
    
- **Knowledge Base (RAG):**
    
    - The knowledge base must _not_ just be a bibliography or list of reference papers; it should be a functional RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) or graph database consumed by your model.
        
    - You are encouraged to embed the RAG into your Agentic AI solution so every step is governed and checked against your collected policies.
        
    - **Bonus Points:** Providing a comparative analysis in your technical report showing how your AI agent behaves _with_ policy governance versus _without_ it will give you a significant advantage.
        
- **Open Source vs. Confidentiality:**
    
    - All policy documents and reference papers **must be openly available and sourced legally** from public domains (no confidential government or corporate materials). 
        
    - Proprietary/closed-source _models_ are allowed, but the code/framework and knowledge bases should ideally leverage open approaches or shareable components where possible.
        
- **Development Environment & Sandboxes:**
      
    - ITU will allocate limited cloud/sandbox resources for selected teams _after_ the first round of evaluation.
        
    - However, using the ITU sandbox is **not mandatory**; teams are fully permitted to use their own development environments and resources right away.
        
## 👥 Teams & Mentorship

- **Cross-Country Collaboration:** Teammates can be from different universities or countries. Teams can choose to focus on the host country (UAE) or their home country's regulations.
     
    
- **Mentorship Support:** If teams need help reviewing submissions or have questions, they can reach out directly to the organizers (Vishnu, Chang, Thomas) via the main WhatsApp group.

## 📊 Mapping Against the AI Readiness Report 2.0 Framework

The primary new topic introduced in this session was mapping solutions to the **13 dimensions** found in **Clause 3 (Structural Approach) of the AI Readiness Report (Version 2.0)**.

Teams are **not** expected to map every single dimension—only select the dimensions that naturally fit and reflect their prototype. Key dimensions to consider for your technical report mapping include:

1. **Data and Model Marketplace:** Describe your metadata, data volumes, variety, collection techniques (e.g., sensors, labs, mobile data), geographical coverage, and data privacy/anonymization standards.
    
2. **Generated Content Marketplace:** (Typically high-level; usually applies more to institutional/national environments than individual agentic solutions).
    
3. **Cross-Domain Correlation Analysis:** Highlight whether your AI solution or core idea can be easily extended and applied to other sectors/domains (e.g., using a mechanism built for plant vibration to detect anomalies elsewhere).
    
4. **Contextualization and Regional Impact:** Explain how your solution accounts for regional properties, local languages/dialects, cultural sensitivities, and local user requirements.
    
5. **Level of Integration & Automation:** Detail the efficiency gains, time saved, scalability, cost factors, and discovery suitability of your integration.
    
6. **Human Interface:** Evaluate the user-friendliness, accessibility, and readiness of the interface for end users.
    
7. **Strategy Alignment:** Address how your solution bridges multiple ministries or stakeholder departments (e.g., Education intersecting with Health or Public Services) and identify potential policy friction points.
    
8. **Collaboration with AI:** Compare human-only workflows versus human-plus-AI workflows, measuring efficiency and prompting overhead.
    
9. **Impact of Humans in AI Integration:** Emphasize the collaboration between domain experts and AI developers, and how domain-specific rules are safely baked into the RAG/knowledge base.
    
10. **AI Policy and Policy for AI:** Incorporate high-level national AI strategies alongside vertical-specific sector regulations. Use this to explicitly point out **policy gaps** where current regulations fail to support advanced AI actions.
    
11. **Inclusion:** Address how your solution targets digital divides, gender gaps, accessibility, or demographic representation (e.g., avatars, accents, local constraints).
    
12. **Regional Priorities:** Align with the core focus domains mandated for the UAE Hackathon.
    
13. **Digital Infrastructure:** Document the underlying hardware, edge vs. core cloud architectures, sensor configurations, and network connectivity used.
